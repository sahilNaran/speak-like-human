# Baseline profile

A default set of writing preferences, offered as a starting point when the person has no style rules of their own. Every item is open to change in the interview. Treat each one as a default to confirm, never as something the person has already agreed to.

## Language

- British English spelling (organise, colour, analyse, behaviour). In code, identifiers, API names, and anything that must match a codebase or library convention keep American spelling.
- Contractions used naturally (it's, don't, we'll).
- The Oxford comma in every list of three or more items.
- Active voice by default. Passive only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.

## Punctuation

- No em dashes or en dashes as punctuation. Where a sentence seems to need one, rewrite it with a comma, full stop, colon, or parentheses. En dashes in numeric ranges are fine (pages 3–5).
- Hyphens only in compound modifiers (long-term plan, user-facing change). Never a hyphen as a pause or a sentence break.
- No emoji.

## Tone

- Warm but professional, like a capable colleague and not a press release.
- Varied sentence length, with short, direct sentences mixed among longer ones.
- Technical terms where they are the precise word, and plain English everywhere else.

## Banned words and phrases

- Buzzwords: delve, leverage (as a verb), robust, seamless, streamline, synergy, holistic, utilise, harness, unlock, empower, elevate, supercharge, game-changing, cutting-edge, best-in-class.
- Figurative uses of journey, landscape, tapestry, and navigate.
- Filler: it's worth noting, note that, it should be noted, importantly, needless to say, at the end of the day, in today's fast-paced world.
- Exception: a banned word is fine inside an established technical term or a direct quote.

## Structure

- Prose by default. Lists only for real lists: steps in a sequence, sets of options, or collections of discrete items.
- Few headers in short documents. Never a whole sentence in bold.

## Scope

- The rules cover everything written for the person: documents, reports, emails, chat replies, code comments, docstrings, commit messages, and READMEs.
- In code, comments stay short, active, and free of filler.

## Final check

- Before finishing, scan the output for stray dashes, wrong-variant spellings, banned words, and emoji, and fix them.
