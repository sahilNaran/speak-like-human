# AI tells

Patterns that make text read as machine-written. The words and phrases here are listed as examples of what to avoid, which is why they appear in this file.

Use this catalogue in three ways: to offer the person choices in the interview, to check drafts against, and to keep your own messages clean. No single item proves anything. It's the density that gives writing away, and people differ in which ones they notice, so let the person decide what to ban.

One caution when applying a ban. Suppressing a pattern often produces its nearest relative: remove em dashes and semicolons or hyphens multiply; ban "delve" and "dig into" appears everywhere. When a banned pattern would have appeared, restructure the sentence so nothing needs to fill the gap.

## 1. Vocabulary

Words that are rare in ordinary writing and common in generated text.

- Verbs: delve, leverage, utilise, harness, unlock, empower, elevate, streamline, foster, underscore, showcase, navigate (figurative), embark, supercharge, spearhead
- Adjectives: robust, seamless, holistic, comprehensive, crucial, pivotal, vibrant, intricate, meticulous, nuanced, multifaceted, cutting-edge, game-changing, best-in-class
- Nouns: landscape, tapestry, journey, realm, synergy, testament, interplay, paradigm, ecosystem (all when figurative)
- Intensifiers and sincerity markers: truly, genuinely, honestly, frankly, incredibly, absolutely, deeply
- Stacked hedges: "could potentially", "may possibly", "might perhaps"

## 2. Openers and closers

- Praise for the question: "Great question", "That's a fascinating point", "Excellent idea"
- Acknowledgement before the answer: "Certainly!", "Absolutely!", "Of course", "I'd be happy to help"
- Announcing what's coming: "Let's dive in", "Here's a breakdown", "Let me walk you through"
- Closers: "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you have any questions", "Feel free to reach out", "Happy to elaborate"
- A final paragraph that restates what was just said: "In summary", "Overall", "In conclusion", "Ultimately"
- A closing line that turns the content into a moral or a quotable phrase

## 3. Filler and signposting

- "It's worth noting that", "It's important to note", "Note that", "It should be noted"
- "Importantly", "Notably", "Interestingly", "Needless to say"
- "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "In terms of", "In today's fast-paced world"
- "Here's the thing", "The truth is", "Let's be clear", "Make no mistake"
- Therapeutic or validating phrases where no one asked for support: "That's completely understandable", "You're not alone in this"

## 4. Sentence patterns

- Setting up a position nobody holds in order to correct it: "It's not just X, it's Y", "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- Reflexive groups of three: three adjectives, three examples, or three clauses, used for the rhythm and not because there are three things
- A colon or a question staged as a reveal: "The result? Chaos.", "Here's what matters: trust."
- A short, punchy sentence ending every paragraph
- Rhetorical questions the writer answers at once
- Sentences that open with "By" plus a gerund and end in a benefit: "By doing X, you can achieve Y"
- Paired balance for its own sake: "both X and Y", "not only X but also Y", "whether X or Y"
- Very even rhythm, where every sentence is about the same length, or alternation so regular it becomes its own pattern

## 5. Punctuation and formatting

- Em dashes used as an all-purpose pause, often several in a paragraph
- Headers on a reply that's only a few paragraphs long
- Bold scattered across phrases, or whole sentences in bold
- Bullets that break continuous reasoning into fragments
- A bold label followed by a colon at the start of each bullet
- Title case in headings
- Emoji as section markers or bullet points
- Typographic curly quotes and arrows in places where a person would have typed plain characters

## 6. Content habits

- Hedging every claim, so that nothing is asserted
- Presenting both sides evenly when the question asked for a view
- Explaining what the reader obviously knows already
- Generic examples where a specific one was available
- Describing what will be covered instead of covering it
- Corporate abstraction: "drive value", "move the needle", "key stakeholders", "actionable insights", "going forward"
- Unprompted caveats and disclaimers
- Enthusiasm that the content doesn't warrant
